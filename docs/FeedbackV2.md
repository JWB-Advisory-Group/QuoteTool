1. First Reaction
I’d be curious. I would not ignore it.

This is better than a random AI quote tool because it actually thinks about floor pricing, drive time, labor, materials, risk, route zone, packages, and follow-up. That matters.

But I would not fully run my business on it yet. Right now it feels like a strong pricing coach with a decent dashboard, not a complete quoting and booking machine.

Would I pay? Maybe a small monthly trial. Not serious money yet.

Compared to QuoteIQ-style intake: better margin logic, weaker booking/conversion workflow.

2. What I Actually Need
I need this to:

Get a quote request in while I’m working.

4. What Is Weak, Confusing, or Unnecessary
The “Approve and book” button is fake right now. It only shows a message. That is bad. If a homeowner clicks approve, I need that to change lead status, notify me, capture preferred dates, and ideally collect a deposit.

The intake asks roof pitch and window details even when I’m only quoting a house wash. That feels sloppy. If I click “screens” on a house wash, the exclusion mentions screens even though windows were not selected. That creates confusion.

Mobile starts with too much hero copy before the form. On a phone, I want the quote form higher.

Permanent lighting and painting should not behave like normal cleaning add-ons. Those need a survey/deposit/manual-estimate path.

6. Pricing Logic Feedback
The pricing engine is pointed the right way, but it is still not sharp enough.

I tested a 2-story ASAP house wash in 11743 with heavy buildup, long hose pull, ladder work, no photos, and uncertain water. It gave me $810-$995, recommended $875, 6.54 labor hours, 2 techs, half day. Not insane, but I would not want that shown too confidently without photos.

Base minimums: good start.

Square footage: okay, but too bucketed.

Linear footage: present for gutters/fences, good.

Windows: too coarse. Count alone is not enough.

Roof: pitch exists, but roof pricing needs stronger walkability/severity/chemical risk.

Surface condition: good idea, too broad.

Chemicals/materials: better than nothing, but severity needs more impact.

Drive time: route zones are useful.

Crew size/job duration: useful, but not tied to actual calendar capacity.

Access risk: good flags, but caps may still underprice ugly jobs.

Urgency: should depend on real schedule load, not just a fixed multiplier.

Bundles: do not discount unless setup time is actually saved.

Deposits: logic exists, but no collection. That is unfinished.
